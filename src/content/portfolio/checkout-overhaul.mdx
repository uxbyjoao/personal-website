## Overview

Nivoda is a global B2B marketplace for diamonds. Throughout 2023, customer feedback channels revealed a pattern of severe usability issues in the web checkout — confused payment terms, unexplained disabled checkouts, and poor post-order communication were driving support tickets and lost sales.

I led the design work across the full human-centred design cycle: heuristic evaluation, usability testing, solution design, and validation — collaborating closely with Product, Research, and Engineering.

## The Challenge

Checkout CSAT averaged **82** over a rolling 12-month period. Customers were:

- Unknowingly breaching credit limits, causing orders to default to unintended payment terms
- Encountering disabled checkouts with no explanation — told only to "contact support"
- Missing that Upfront Pay required payment before order confirmation, leading to 24-hour timeouts and sold-out stock
- Unable to orient themselves in the checkout flow due to a broken step indicator

## Formative Research

**Heuristic evaluation** — Workshops analysing the existing flow against Nielsen's 10 heuristics surfaced violations in system status visibility, consistency, minimalist design, and real-world language match.

**Usability testing** — 11 real buyers walked through the checkout in moderated think-aloud sessions, directly surfacing the problems that shaped the redesign.

**Benchmarking** — Desk research using the Baymard Institute and Mobbin established industry best practices for pricing display, step indicators, and information hierarchy.

These activities converged into **10 prioritised problem statements**, each with evidence, proposed solutions, expected impact, and effort estimates.

## Hypothesis

Addressing the 10 problem statements would measurably improve the checkout experience, tracked against:

- **CSAT** — Baseline 12-month rolling average of **82**; target: meaningful uplift
- **Support ticket volume** — Reducing avoidable contacts caused by unexplained disabled checkouts and unintended payment term defaults
- **Sold-out rate** — Reducing order timeouts from customers who didn't realise Upfront Pay required payment before confirmation

## Proposed Solution

I designed a high-fidelity Figma prototype addressing all 10 problem statements:

- **Payment terms screen** — Clear credit availability messaging and explanation of each payment option
- **Fail-state messaging** — Specific, actionable messages for missing KYC, low credit, and overdue invoices instead of generic "contact support"
- **Cart review** — Support for Express, returns, Canada Mark, notes, and stone dimensions
- **Delivery selection** — Explicit single vs. multiple address options
- **Order confirmation** — Clear next-step communication and calls-to-action
- **Step indicator** — Fixed progress indicator so users always know where they are

## Validation

The design was validated through cross-functional review with Product, Finance, and Engineering — including detailed feedback from Finance on payment term logic and credit card flows. During final QA, cross-category compatibility testing caught that the new design needed adjustments for Melee (small diamond) orders, which was resolved with a brief design iteration before launch.

## Implementation

Design was handed off in February 2024. Frontend development was led by a dedicated engineer with QA following a DevBranch regression process. The launch was briefly delayed to accommodate melee compatibility fixes and a final round of regression testing. Released to production on **9 April 2024**.

## Results

In the first full month post-launch:

- **CSAT rose to 90** — an 8-point improvement over the 12-month average of **82**, crossing from "Good" into "Excellent" on the benchmark scale
- **Support ticket drivers shifted** — Post-launch negative feedback was about external factors (sold-out stones, supplier delivery times) rather than checkout usability, confirming the core UX problems were resolved
- **Negative-to-positive ratio**: 13 negative vs. 312 positive responses (4% negative rate)

The overhaul also unblocked downstream initiatives including Memo checkout flows, mobile checkout planning, and the Pay at Checkout initiative.
